Abstract

Video dubbing replaces spoken content while preserving the person, performance, and scene of a source video. Existing systems are predominantly trained and evaluated on short, same-audio captured clips. This protocol creates three deployment mismatches: the recorded mouth is invalid supervision under replacement speech, captured clips do not represent web and generated sources, and short evaluations do not reveal long-execution instability. We introduce CausalDub, which first learns a full-context replacement response and then transfers it to a causal two-step editor. Its asymmetric supervision retains full reconstruction for same-audio samples but removes source-mouth targets under replacement audio while preserving valid non-mouth evidence. Guided causal adaptation and trajectory regression transfer the learned response into a two-evaluation autoregressive student. Distribution matching recovers compression-induced visual degradation, while source-reanchored windows bound generated-state propagation on long inputs. We also introduce CausalDub-Bench, comprising 256 source-disjoint generated videos with fixed replacement-audio pairs, and evaluate captured, web, generated, and 60-second sources. Across four benchmarks, matched replacement-aware refinement improves cross-audio Sync-C by 0.954–1.653 with mixed FVD, and, among evaluated external methods, the final model attains the highest cross-audio Sync-C on every benchmark.

Method

The method separates source preservation from replacement-speech control, then transfers a guided parent response into a two-step causal student.

Masked source and reference preserve appearance; frame-local audio controls articulation; block-causal attention reads only committed generated history.

  • Causal adaptation — convert the bidirectional editor so each block only sees past video state.
  • Trajectory distillation — regress a two-step initializer from the guided causal parent.
  • Guided DMD + retention — match the teacher distribution while anchoring non-mouth appearance.
  • One-branch inference — replacement speech at 2 NFE without a second classifier-free branch at test time.

Counterfactual adaptation, matched trajectory regression, and guided distribution matching with retention. Auxiliary networks are removed at inference.
